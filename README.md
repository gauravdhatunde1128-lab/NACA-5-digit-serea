✈️ NACA 5-Digit Airfoil Description

📌 A NACA 5-digit airfoil is represented by a five-digit number in which each digit provides information about the airfoil’s aerodynamic characteristics. The first digit indicates the design lift coefficient, which is equal to 0.15 times the first digit. The second and third digits define the camber-line family and determine the position and shape of the maximum camber. The last two digits represent the maximum thickness of the airfoil as a percentage of the chord length.

🔹 For example, in NACA 23012, the first digit “2” corresponds to a design lift coefficient of 0.3, the digits “30” specify the 230-series camber line, and the last two digits “12” indicate that the maximum thickness is 12% of the chord length.

🚀 Compared with NACA 4-digit airfoils, NACA 5-digit airfoils use a more advanced camber-line design to provide higher lift, improved aerodynamic efficiency, and better performance at the design lift coefficient. These airfoils were developed to meet specific aerodynamic requirements and are widely used in aircraft wing design and aerodynamic research.

📖 Example: NACA 23012

* ✨ Design Lift Coefficient = 0.3
* ✨ Camber Line Family = 230 Series
* ✨ Maximum Thickness = 12% of Chord Length

🎯 Advantages of NACA 5-Digit Airfoils

* ✅ Higher design lift coefficient
* ✅ Better aerodynamic efficiency
* ✅ Improved lift characteristics
* ✅ Suitable for aircraft requiring specific performance goals
* ✅ More advanced than many NACA 4-digit airfoils

🛩️ NACA 5-digit airfoils represent an important step in airfoil development and are commonly studied in aeronautical engineering for their improved aerodynamic performance and design methodology.
